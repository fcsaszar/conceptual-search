* Step 0: Initial state

## One-Sentence Summary
A privacy-first platform that generates verifiable digital fingerprints of ethical sourcing events and anchors them to a public, machine-readable ledger, giving brands, NGOs, and consumers indisputable proof of provenance while protecting sensitive data.

## Problem
The venture plans to address a digital landscape saturated with manipulation and duplication, where it is increasingly difficult to establish what is original or real. The central challenge is enabling people to credibly prove the authenticity and first possession of a digital record—such as a donation confirmation or an impact report—amid pervasive skepticism and the ease of generating false claims. A specific, related challenge the venture plans to explore is the inefficiency and lack of standardization in the verification process for corporate matching gift programs, where employees often face cumbersome manual steps to prove their donations, creating friction that reduces participation.

## Target Users & Use Cases
The venture plans to target both brands seeking to validate their supply chains and consumers looking for trustworthy ethical goods. A planned, specific target user group for initial outreach is procurement teams within consumer goods companies participating in sustainability certification or reporting programs, who need a streamlined way to discover vetted suppliers and provide indisputable proof of their ethical sourcing actions to qualify for certifications or meet regulatory requirements. The venture also plans to explore targeting non-governmental organizations (NGOs) and impact-focused philanthropic foundations as a distinct user group, who require independently verifiable, tamper-proof records of field-level sourcing events and material provenance to substantiate their impact reports to donors, auditors, and grant-making bodies.

## Solution / Product Overview
The venture plans to build a tool that creates a digital fingerprint of a user's charitable event data—such as a donation receipt or a nonprofit's impact milestone—and records proof of that fingerprint on a public, time-stamped ledger. The approach is proposed to be secure and privacy-preserving: users would not upload sensitive raw data; instead, a compact fingerprint would serve as durable evidence. This is positioned as a quiet safeguard for digital truth that donors, nonprofits, and verifying partners can rely on. To increase trust, the team plans to explore adding a decentralized, community-run witness network to provide a secondary, distributed layer of attestation to the ledger's timestamps. Furthermore, the venture plans to explore the development of a simple, open-source verification widget that any third-party website (like a nonprofit's blog or a corporate social responsibility page) could embed to allow visitors to independently verify the timestamp and authenticity of a claimed charitable fingerprint directly from that page. Additionally, the venture plans to explore a feature where users could link multiple related fingerprints (e.g., a donation receipt, a supplier invoice, and a shipping confirmation) into a single, verifiable provenance bundle, creating a more comprehensive audit trail for complex ethical sourcing events.

## Delivery Model
The venture plans a lightweight, privacy-first implementation. It would not store sensitive donor financial details or the underlying event files. Instead, it would generate compact, anonymized fingerprints of that data for ledger anchoring. The goal is a secure and resource-conscious design that maintains long-term verifiability with minimal infrastructure. The team plans to build a clean public interface and supporting infrastructure to make the verification tools usable by its target audience.

## Revenue Logic
The venture plans to generate revenue through the sale of a subscription service to brands and suppliers for access to the verification ledger, advanced provenance reporting features, and supply chain management capabilities within the platform. An initial free trial period for the subscription service is proposed to onboard supplier and brand partners. The venture also plans to explore potential optional upgrades for enhanced connectivity or integration services for enterprise procurement or certification programs.

## Distinctiveness
The venture aims to differentiate itself by offering heirloom-quality goods that merge immediate, tangible material qualities with enduring digital proof of ethical sourcing. Its proposed system does not just provide a product; it aims to create a permanent, independently verifiable record that the materials were sourced according to specific ethical standards at a specific time, addressing the credibility problem of sustainability claims. This focus on verifiable provenance from a specific, ethical source is presented as a unique safeguard for both consumer trust and material truth. The venture plans to explore a further point of differentiation: structuring its public ledger not just as a tamper-proof record, but as a machine-readable, open registry of ethical sourcing standards. This would allow the fingerprints to be programmatically linked to the precise definitions of the ethical standards they claim to uphold, aiming to create a new layer of semantic clarity and auditability that is missing from current provenance claims.

The approach is proposed to be secure and privacy-preserving: users would not upload sensitive raw data; instead, a compact fingerprint would serve as durable evidence. This is positioned as a quiet safeguard for digital truth that donors, nonprofits, and verifying partners can rely on. To increase trust, the team plans to explore adding a decentralized, community-run witness network to provide a secondary, distributed layer of attestation to the ledger's timestamps. To demonstrate the proposed machine-readable registry, the venture plans to explore two specific, initial test cases. The first would involve programmatically linking fingerprints to the "Fair Trade Certified™" standard. As part of this exploration, the venture plans to pursue a partnership discussion with a specific Fair Trade licensing body or standard-holder to discuss the technical linking of fingerprints to their official standard definitions. The second proposed test case would explore generating verifiable fingerprints for corporate volunteer hour matching programs. This test is planned to directly demonstrate applicability to the mentioned matching gift challenge, but its scope and design are planned to be informed by the proposed discussion with a corporate matching gift program administrator. Both planned explorations would aim to allow a fingerprint to be automatically associated with the precise, public definition of the standard or program criteria it claims to uphold. Furthermore, the venture plans to explore the development of a simple, open-source verification widget that any third-party website could embed to allow visitors to independently verify the timestamp, authenticity, and linked standard of a claimed fingerprint directly from that page. The venture plans to propose a small-scale, controlled test of this open-source verification widget with a university's corporate social responsibility page or a student-run impact blog to gather initial usability feedback. Additionally, the venture plans to explore a feature where users could link multiple related fingerprints into a single, verifiable provenance bundle, creating a more comprehensive audit trail for complex ethical sourcing events. To ensure these bundles are universally interpretable, the venture also plans to propose the development of a clear, public schema for structuring and linking these multi-fingerprint provenance bundles. The team plans to publish a draft of this proposed schema for open community feedback.
