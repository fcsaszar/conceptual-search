* Step 0: Initial state

## One-Sentence Summary

A line of heirloom jewelry uses real leather that is cultivated in bioreactors from a one-time, harmless skin-cell sample taken from a living, sanctuary-grazing cow, aiming to offer animal-friendly leather adornments.

## Problem

The creators frame conventional leather as dependent on animal harm and present a gap for ethically sourced, real leather that preserves the material's qualities without sacrificing animal welfare. They position the project as evidence that leather can be made while the donor animal continues to live and be cared for at a sanctuary.

## Solution / Product Overview

The project offers jewelry featuring inlays of cultivated leather grown from the donor cow's skin cells on a plant-based scaffold. The leather is produced in bioreactors and then vegetable-tanned, allowing the creators to present finished wearable pieces while asserting that no additional animal harvesting is required.

## Key Features & Capabilities

-   Real leather cultivated from living-animal skin cells grown on a plant-derived scaffold.

-   Inlays set within metal bezels and protected under a glass dome cabochon.

-   Options to choose hypoallergenic stainless steel or sterling silver for certain pieces.

-   Offered in multiple size, color, and material combinations.

-   Packaged in eco-friendly boxes cushioned with plant seed fiber used in the cultivation process.

## Target Users & Use Cases

The campaign speaks to people seeking animal-friendly, sustainable jewelry and those interested in owning a first-of-its-kind heirloom piece made from cultivated leather grown from a specific donor animal's cells.

## Development Status

The team shows finished jewelry inlays and describes past milestones: cells were isolated harmlessly in 2023, prototypes include a pendant and a card holder made in early 2025, and the leather is grown with patent-pending plant-based scaffolding. They state there are no outside approvals required and that the technology and cell lines are owned by the creators.

## Roadmap & Delivery Plan

Planned actions include pilot testing to tune color and texture, inspecting scaffold density before use, preparing different concentrations of plant-based tanning ingredients, and securing jewelry settings after style counts are finalized at the end of the campaign. Production scaling will be addressed by adjusting modular cultivated-leather bioreactor technology and resources.

## Why Crowdfunding / Use of Funds

Funds support bringing the cultivated-leather jewelry to backers: producing inlays, performing pilot tests for consistency, and procuring jewelry settings and sustainable packaging to deliver finished pieces.

## Risks & Challenges

Stated risks include: loss of cryopreserved cells (mitigated by storage at multiple facilities); variability in final color and surface texture from vegetable tanning and scaffold differences (addressed through pilot testing and scaffold inspection); potential supply-chain changes requiring alternate jewelry settings of equal or better quality; and possible extended delivery times for later backers if demand is high.

## Team & Partnerships

The effort is led by a founder with long-term tissue-engineering experience and cellular-agriculture leadership since 2018, who worked with a sanctuary and veterinary professionals to isolate the donor cells.

## What's Included

Jewelry pieces with cultivated-leather inlays (e.g., a rectangular inlay approximately 10 mm × 50 mm) and, for some items, a waxed linen cord; components are housed in metal bezels and glass cabochons.

## Technical Details

-   Plant-based scaffold from kapok seed fibers, needle-felted.

-   Surface treatment with plant-derived ingredients (e.g., sunflower lecithin, zein protein) to promote cell attachment.

-   Cells produce collagen that becomes vegetable-tanned leather.

-   Approximate inlay dimension: 10 mm × 50 mm.
