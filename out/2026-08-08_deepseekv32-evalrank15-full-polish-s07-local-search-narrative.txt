* Step 0: Initial state

## One-Sentence Summary
A privacy-first platform that creates permanent, verifiable digital fingerprints of ethical sourcing events anchored to a public ledger, building an interactive ecosystem with educational tools for supply chain professionals and impact-focused organizations.

## Problem
The venture plans to address a digital landscape saturated with manipulation and duplication, where it is increasingly difficult to establish what is original or real. The central challenge is enabling people to credibly prove the authenticity and first possession of a digital record—such as a donation confirmation or an impact report—amid pervasive skepticism and the ease of generating false claims. A specific, related challenge the venture plans to explore is the inefficiency and lack of standardization in the verification process for corporate matching gift programs, where employees often face cumbersome manual steps to prove their donations, creating friction that reduces participation. The venture also plans to explore addressing the problem of 'greenwashing' and unsubstantiated ethical claims in consumer markets, where brands make sustainability assertions that are difficult for consumers, regulators, and even the brands' own procurement teams to independently verify against original sourcing events, leading to market confusion and eroded trust.

## Target Users & Use Cases
The venture plans to target both brands seeking to validate their supply chains and consumers looking for trustworthy ethical goods. A planned, specific target user group for initial outreach is procurement teams within consumer goods companies participating in sustainability certification or reporting programs, who need a streamlined way to discover vetted suppliers and provide indisputable proof of their ethical sourcing actions to qualify for certifications or meet regulatory requirements. The venture also plans to explore targeting non-governmental organizations (NGOs) and impact-focused philanthropic foundations as a distinct user group, who require independently verifiable, tamper-proof records of field-level sourcing events and material provenance to substantiate their impact reports to donors, auditors, and grant-making bodies.

## Solution / Product Overview
The venture plans to build a tool that creates a digital fingerprint of a user's charitable event data—such as a donation receipt or a nonprofit's impact milestone—and records proof of that fingerprint on a public, time-stamped ledger. The approach is proposed to be secure and privacy-preserving: users would not upload sensitive raw data; instead, a compact fingerprint would serve as durable evidence. This is positioned as a quiet safeguard for digital truth that donors, nonprofits, and verifying partners can rely on. To increase trust, the team plans to explore adding a decentralized, community-run witness network to provide a secondary, distributed layer of attestation to the ledger's timestamps. Furthermore, the venture plans to explore the development of a simple, open-source verification widget that any third-party website (like a nonprofit's blog or a corporate social responsibility page) could embed to allow visitors to independently verify the timestamp and authenticity of a claimed charitable fingerprint directly from that page.

## Delivery Model
The venture plans a lightweight, privacy-first implementation. It would not store sensitive donor financial details or the underlying event files. Instead, it would generate compact, anonymized fingerprints of that data for ledger anchoring. The goal is a secure and resource-conscious design that maintains long-term verifiability with minimal infrastructure. The team plans to build a clean public interface and supporting infrastructure to make the verification tools usable by its target audience.

## Revenue Logic
The venture plans to generate revenue through the sale of a subscription service to brands and suppliers for access to the verification ledger, advanced provenance reporting features, and supply chain management capabilities within the platform. An initial free trial period for the subscription service is proposed to onboard supplier and brand partners. The venture also plans to explore potential optional upgrades for enhanced connectivity or integration services for enterprise procurement or certification programs.

## Distinctiveness
The venture aims to differentiate itself by offering heirloom-quality goods that merge immediate, tangible material qualities with enduring digital proof of ethical sourcing. Its proposed system does not just provide a product; it aims to create a permanent, independently verifiable record that the materials were sourced according to specific ethical standards at a specific time, addressing the credibility problem of sustainability claims. This focus on verifiable provenance from a specific, ethical source is presented as a unique safeguard for both consumer trust and material truth. The venture plans to explore a further point of differentiation: structuring its public ledger not just as a tamper-proof record, but as a machine-readable, open registry of ethical sourcing standards. This would allow the fingerprints to be programmatically linked to the precise definitions of the ethical standards they claim to uphold, aiming to create a new layer of semantic clarity and auditability that is missing from current provenance claims.

## One-Sentence Summary
A privacy-first platform that creates permanent, verifiable digital fingerprints of ethical sourcing events anchored to a public ledger, building an interactive ecosystem with educational tools for supply chain professionals and impact-focused organizations.

## Problem
The venture plans to address a digital landscape saturated with manipulation and duplication, where it is increasingly difficult to establish what is original or real. The central challenge is enabling people to credibly prove the authenticity and first possession of a digital record—such as a donation confirmation or an impact report—amid pervasive skepticism and the ease of generating false claims. A specific, related challenge the venture plans to explore is the inefficiency and lack of standardization in the verification process for corporate matching gift programs, where employees often face cumbersome manual steps to prove their donations, creating friction that reduces participation. The venture also plans to explore addressing the problem of 'greenwashing' and unsubstantiated ethical claims in consumer markets, where brands make sustainability assertions that are difficult for consumers, regulators, and even the brands' own procurement teams to independently verify against original sourcing events, leading to market confusion and eroded trust.

## Target Users & Use Cases
The venture plans to target both brands seeking to validate their supply chains and consumers looking for trustworthy ethical goods. A planned, specific target user group for initial outreach is procurement teams within consumer goods companies participating in sustainability certification or reporting programs, who need a streamlined way to discover vetted suppliers and provide indisputable proof of their ethical sourcing actions to qualify for certifications or meet regulatory requirements. The venture also plans to explore targeting non-governmental organizations (NGOs) and impact-focused philanthropic foundations as a distinct user group, who require independently verifiable, tamper-proof records of field-level sourcing events and material provenance to substantiate their impact reports to donors, auditors, and grant-making bodies.

To clarify the workflow for this key user group, the venture plans to design a simple user journey for procurement teams. Before building, the venture plans to conduct structured interviews with 10-15 procurement professionals from this target user group to refine the proposed journey. The planned, interview-informed journey would illustrate the steps from: (1) discovering a vetted supplier within the platform's registry; (2) initiating a sourcing transaction and receiving standard documentation (e.g., invoices, certification proofs); (3) using the platform's tool to generate a compact, anonymized digital fingerprint of the sourcing event data; (4) anchoring that fingerprint to the public, time-stamped ledger to create a permanent record; and (5) seamlessly integrating the resulting verifiable proof into their internal compliance reports or public-facing sustainability claims. To validate that the proposed 'anonymized digital fingerprint' actually meets the privacy and compliance requirements of target companies (e.g., GDPR, trade secret concerns), the venture plans to conduct a specific test within this user journey. This planned test would involve collaborating with legal and compliance officers from the interviewed companies to review the fingerprint generation methodology and data outputs, ensuring they contain no sensitive or personally identifiable information while still providing a unique, verifiable link to the original event.

## Solution / Product Overview
The venture plans to build a tool that creates a digital fingerprint of a user's charitable event data—such as a donation receipt or a nonprofit's impact milestone—and records proof of that fingerprint on a public, time-stamped ledger. The approach is proposed to be secure and privacy-preserving: users would not upload sensitive raw data; instead, a compact fingerprint would serve as durable evidence. This is positioned as a quiet safeguard for digital truth that donors, nonprofits, and verifying partners can rely on. To add concreteness to the 'secure anchoring' claim for its planned technical proof-of-concept, the venture plans to explore using the IOTA Tangle as the specific, initial public ledger technology. This is proposed as an immutable data structure to validate the anchoring mechanism. To increase trust, the team plans to explore adding a decentralized, community-run witness network to provide a secondary, distributed layer of attestation to the ledger's timestamps. Furthermore, the venture plans to explore the development of a simple, open-source verification widget that any third-party website (like a nonprofit's blog or a corporate social responsibility page) could embed to allow visitors to independently verify the timestamp and authenticity of a claimed charitable fingerprint directly from that page.

## Delivery Model
The venture plans a lightweight, privacy-first implementation. It would not store sensitive donor financial details or the underlying event files. Instead, it would generate compact, anonymized fingerprints of that data for ledger anchoring. The goal is a secure and resource-conscious design that maintains long-term verifiability with minimal infrastructure. The team plans to build a clean public interface and supporting infrastructure to make the verification tools usable by its target audience. To de-risk the core technology, the venture plans to first execute a small-scale technical proof-of-concept. This planned proof-of-concept is designed to validate the proposed digital fingerprint generation algorithm and its successful, secure anchoring to the proposed public ledger (the IOTA Tangle) using simulated event data, before any pilot with live users or data.

## Revenue Logic
The venture plans to generate revenue through the sale of a subscription service to brands and suppliers. To make this logic more tangible, the venture plans to explore a simple tiered subscription model. The planned model would include a Basic tier providing core verification access to the public ledger; a Pro tier adding advanced provenance reporting and supply chain management features; and an Enterprise tier offering API integration and enhanced connectivity services for enterprise procurement or certification programs. An initial free trial period for the subscription service is proposed to onboard supplier and brand partners.

## Distinctiveness
The venture aims to differentiate itself by offering heirloom-quality goods that merge immediate, tangible material qualities with enduring digital proof of ethical sourcing. Its proposed system does not just provide a product; it aims to create a permanent, independently verifiable record that the materials were sourced according to specific ethical standards at a specific time, addressing the credibility problem of sustainability claims. This focus on verifiable provenance from a specific, ethical source is presented as a unique safeguard for both consumer trust and material truth. The venture plans to explore a further point of differentiation: structuring its public ledger not just as a tamper-proof record, but as a machine-readable, open registry of ethical sourcing standards. This would allow the fingerprints to be programmatically linked to the precise definitions of the ethical standards they claim to uphold, aiming to create a new layer of semantic clarity and auditability that is missing from current provenance claims.

## Planned Validation
The venture plans to conduct a small-scale pilot test to validate its approach to reducing friction in specific use cases. This planned pilot is to explore a collaboration with one university's employee giving program to test the platform's ability to streamline the verification process for corporate matching gift donations. A specific, proposed success metric for this pilot is a target of 50% reduction in average verification time per claim compared to the program's existing manual baseline. In parallel, the venture plans to conduct a small, concurrent validation test with a single local artisan or farm cooperative to generate a real 'ethical sourcing event' fingerprint. This planned test is intended to demonstrate the core use case for supply chain provenance alongside the donation pilot. Furthermore, the venture plans to explore a proposed collaboration with one sustainability certification body to test the integration of the platform's verifiable proofs into their audit process, framed as a pilot to validate the regulatory use case. This planned pilot with a certification body is intended to be a distinct test with defined success metrics, such as a planned measurement of reduction in audit time for specific verification steps and planned feedback collection on auditor satisfaction with the verifiable proof format. These planned pilot tests are intended to follow the successful completion of the proposed technical proof-of-concept for the digital fingerprint and ledger anchoring system.
