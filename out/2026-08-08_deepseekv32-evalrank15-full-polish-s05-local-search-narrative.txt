## One-Sentence Summary
A privacy-first platform that generates verifiable digital fingerprints of ethical sourcing events and anchors them to a public, tamper-proof ledger, giving brands and consumers indisputable proof of provenance while protecting sensitive operational data.

## Problem
The venture plans to address a digital landscape saturated with manipulation and duplication, where it is increasingly difficult to establish what is original or real. The central challenge is enabling people to credibly prove the authenticity and first possession of a digital record—such as a donation confirmation or an impact report—amid pervasive skepticism and the ease of generating false claims. A specific, related challenge the venture plans to explore is the inefficiency and lack of standardization in the verification process for corporate matching gift programs, where employees often face cumbersome manual steps to prove their donations, creating friction that reduces participation.

## Target Users & Use Cases
The venture plans to target both brands seeking to validate their supply chains and consumers looking for trustworthy ethical goods. Primary scenarios include discovering vetted, ethical suppliers (from large, global operations to local, artisanal producers), verifying material provenance with confidence, and providing transparent proof of sourcing standards to end customers. A planned, specific target user group for initial outreach is procurement teams within consumer goods companies participating in sustainability certification or reporting programs, who need a streamlined way to discover vetted suppliers and provide indisputable proof of their ethical sourcing actions to qualify for certifications or meet regulatory requirements.

## Solution / Product Overview
The venture plans to build a tool that creates a digital fingerprint of a user's charitable event data—such as a donation receipt or a nonprofit's impact milestone—and records proof of that fingerprint on a public, time-stamped ledger. The approach is proposed to be secure and privacy-preserving: users would not upload sensitive raw data; instead, a compact fingerprint would serve as durable evidence. This is positioned as a quiet safeguard for digital truth that donors, nonprofits, and verifying partners can rely on. To increase trust, the team plans to explore adding a decentralized, community-run witness network to provide a secondary, distributed layer of attestation to the ledger's timestamps. Furthermore, the venture plans to explore the development of a simple, open-source verification widget that any third-party website (like a nonprofit's blog or a corporate social responsibility page) could embed to allow visitors to independently verify the timestamp and authenticity of a claimed charitable fingerprint directly from that page.

## Delivery Model
The venture plans a lightweight, privacy-first implementation. It would not store sensitive donor financial details or the underlying event files. Instead, it would generate compact, anonymized fingerprints of that data for ledger anchoring. The goal is a secure and resource-conscious design that maintains long-term verifiability with minimal infrastructure. The team plans to build a clean public interface and supporting infrastructure to make the verification tools usable by its target audience.

## Revenue Logic
The venture plans to generate revenue primarily through a transaction-based model, charging a fee for each verifiable digital fingerprint anchored to the public ledger. This would apply to brands, suppliers, and potentially other entities (such as nonprofits in the matching gift use case) seeking to create a permanent, auditable record of an ethical sourcing event or donation. The venture also plans to explore offering optional subscription tiers for high-volume users, which could include advanced provenance reporting features, supply chain management capabilities, or enhanced integration services for enterprise procurement or certification programs. An initial free trial period for generating a limited number of fingerprints is proposed to onboard partners.

## Distinctiveness
The venture aims to differentiate itself by offering heirloom-quality goods that merge immediate, tangible material qualities with enduring digital proof of ethical sourcing. Its proposed system does not just provide a product; it aims to create a permanent, independently verifiable record that the materials were sourced according to specific ethical standards at a specific time, addressing the credibility problem of sustainability claims. This focus on verifiable provenance from a specific, ethical source is presented as a unique safeguard for both consumer trust and material truth. The venture plans to explore a further point of differentiation: structuring its public ledger not just as a tamper-proof record, but as a machine-readable, open registry of ethical sourcing standards. This would allow the fingerprints to be programmatically linked to the precise definitions of the ethical standards they claim to uphold, aiming to create a new layer of semantic clarity and auditability that is missing from current provenance claims.

The central challenge is enabling people to credibly prove the authenticity and first possession of a digital record—such as a donation confirmation or an impact report—amid pervasive skepticism and the ease of generating false claims. A specific, related challenge the venture plans to explore is the inefficiency and lack of standardization in the verification process for corporate matching gift programs, where employees often face cumbersome manual steps to prove their donations, creating friction that reduces participation.

## Target Users & Use Cases
The venture plans to target both brands seeking to validate their supply chains and consumers looking for trustworthy ethical goods. Primary scenarios include discovering vetted, ethical suppliers (from large, global operations to local, artisanal producers), verifying material provenance with confidence, and providing transparent proof of sourcing standards to end customers. A planned, specific target user group for initial outreach is procurement teams within consumer goods companies participating in sustainability certification or reporting programs, who need a streamlined way to discover vetted suppliers and provide indisputable proof of their ethical sourcing actions to qualify for certifications or meet regulatory requirements.

## Solution / Product Overview
The venture plans to build a tool that creates a digital fingerprint of a user's charitable event data—such as a donation receipt or a nonprofit's impact milestone—and records proof of that fingerprint on a public, time-stamped ledger. The venture plans to use a specific public ledger technology for anchoring: the Hedera public network. This approach is proposed to be secure and privacy-preserving: users would not upload sensitive raw data; instead, a compact fingerprint would serve as durable evidence. This is positioned as a quiet safeguard for digital truth that donors, nonprofits, and verifying partners can rely on. To increase trust, the team plans to explore adding a decentralized, community-run witness network to provide a secondary, distributed layer of attestation to the ledger's timestamps. To mitigate technical feasibility risk for this network, the venture plans to conduct a dedicated research phase to evaluate a specific existing protocol or framework, such as a decentralized oracle network, for suitability. Furthermore, the venture plans to explore the development of a simple, open-source verification widget that any third-party website (like a nonprofit's blog or a corporate social responsibility page) could embed to allow visitors to independently verify the timestamp and authenticity of a claimed charitable fingerprint directly from that page. To validate the donation verification use case and gather initial feedback on user friction reduction, the venture plans to propose a specific, hypothetical pilot with a single corporate matching gift program administrator, such as a large tech company's HR department. This pilot would aim to measure key friction points, including the average time required for an employee to verify a donation, with a proposed target of reducing this average verification time by 50%, and any change in employee participation rate in the matching gift program, with a proposed target of achieving a 10% increase in participation rate. Feedback would be gathered through anonymized user surveys and analysis of verification process logs. To validate the open-source verification widget, the venture plans to propose a simple, hypothetical test by embedding it on a single, identified nonprofit partner's blog post to measure visitor engagement and trust signals (e.g., click-through rate on the widget).

## Delivery Model
The venture plans a lightweight, privacy-first implementation anchored to the Hedera public ledger. It would not store sensitive donor financial details or the underlying event files. Instead, it would generate compact, anonymized fingerprints of that data for ledger anchoring. The goal is a secure and resource-conscious design that maintains long-term verifiability with minimal infrastructure. The team plans to build a clean public interface and supporting infrastructure to make the verification tools usable by its target audience.

## Revenue Logic
The venture plans to generate revenue primarily through a transaction-based model, charging a fee for each verifiable digital fingerprint anchored to the public ledger. This would apply to brands, suppliers, and potentially other entities (such as nonprofits in the matching gift use case) seeking to create a permanent, auditable record of an ethical sourcing event or donation. The venture plans to propose a specific, tiered pricing structure to clarify the model for enterprise customers. This structure could include, for example, a proposed 'Freemium' tier for the smallest users (e.g., '0-5 fingerprints per month at no cost') to lower the barrier to entry for testing, a standard per-fingerprint cost for low-volume users, a discounted rate for a medium-volume band, and a custom enterprise rate for high-volume bands with significant annual commitments. To make this model more concrete, the venture plans to propose illustrative price bands such as: 'Freemium: 0-5 fingerprints per month at no cost; Standard: $5 per fingerprint for 1-100 events; Medium: $3 per fingerprint for 101-1000 events; Enterprise: custom quote for 1000+ events'. The venture also plans to explore offering optional subscription tiers for high-volume users, which could include advanced provenance reporting features, supply chain management capabilities, or enhanced integration services for enterprise procurement or certification programs. An initial free trial period for generating a limited number of fingerprints is proposed to onboard partners.

## Distinctiveness
The venture aims to differentiate itself by offering heirloom-quality goods that merge immediate, tangible material qualities with enduring digital proof of ethical sourcing. Its proposed system does not just provide a product; it aims to create a permanent, independently verifiable record that the materials were sourced according to specific ethical standards at a specific time, addressing the credibility problem of sustainability claims. This focus on verifiable provenance from a specific, ethical source is presented as a unique safeguard for both consumer trust and material truth. The venture plans to explore a further point of differentiation: structuring its public ledger not just as a tamper-proof record, but as a machine-readable, open registry of ethical sourcing standards. This would allow the fingerprints to be programmatically linked to the precise definitions of the ethical standards they claim to uphold, aiming to create a new layer of semantic clarity and auditability that is missing from current provenance claims. To concretely demonstrate this semantic clarity, the venture plans to prototype a single, specific example of a machine-readable registry entry, such as one for a common standard like Fair Trade, to illustrate how a fingerprint can be linked to the exact criteria, version, and certifying body of the standard it attests to. To validate the machine-readable registry concept and the supplier discovery use case, the venture plans to propose a specific, hypothetical partnership with a single, identified sustainability certification body (e.g., Fair Trade International, B Corp) to explore integrating the machine-readable registry for their standard as a validation test. To validate the supplier discovery use case in a controlled setting, the venture plans to propose a simple, hypothetical test partnership with a single, identified type of entity, such as a local Fair Trade coffee roaster.
