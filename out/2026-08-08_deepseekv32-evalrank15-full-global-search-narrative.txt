## One-Sentence Summary

A lightweight, privacy-first tool that generates a verifiable fingerprint of a file and anchors it to a public, time-stamped ledger so anyone can prove originality and authenticity.

## Problem

The creators describe a digital landscape saturated with deepfakes, fake screenshots, copied artwork, and altered media, where "anyone can post anything," making it increasingly difficult to establish what's original or real. They frame the central question as how people can credibly prove ownership or first possession of digital work amid pervasive manipulation and duplication.

## Solution / Product Overview

The product creates a digital fingerprint of a user's file and records proof of that fingerprint on a public, time-stamped ledger. The approach is presented as secure and privacy-preserving: users do not upload the actual file; instead, a compact fingerprint is used as a durable evidence marker. The creators position this as a quiet safeguard for digital truth that everyday people, as well as professionals, can rely on.

## Key Features & Capabilities

-   File fingerprinting: produces a unique hash of a file for proof of originality.
-   Public, time-stamped anchoring: writes proof to a ledger that can be independently checked.
-   Privacy-first design: only hashed fingerprints are stored, not the underlying files.
-   Lightweight implementation: designed to be efficient and resource-conscious.
-   Security emphasis: presented as "lightweight, secure, and built for the future."

## Target Users & Use Cases

The campaign calls out artists, small businesses, journalists, and anyone who wants to prove "I had this first." Primary uses include establishing authorship, deterring copycats, and documenting the creation time of digital assets.

## Development Status

The "core protocol already works," and an early form is live privately. The team is seeking to build a cleaner public interface and supporting infrastructure so the tool is usable by a broader audience.

## Roadmap & Delivery Plan

Planned steps include hiring a developer and a designer to build a smoother public interface; polishing usability; and layering in infrastructure for scale. The team references a January beta target and says cushion time has been built into the schedule.

## Why Crowdfunding / Use of Funds

## Risks & Challenges

The main risk is talent acquisition: delays in hiring or extended timelines could push back the January beta. Mitigations include existing architecture, a lean scope, added schedule buffer, and a commitment to communicate changes clearly.

## Team & Partnerships

The effort is led by engineers who emphasize building for everyday creators in their lives. All architecture and content are created and reviewed by a human team.

## Technical Details

Only hashed fingerprints are stored, dramatically reducing storage and compute compared with systems that store full files or rely on energy-intensive consensus mechanisms. The goal is long-term security and transparency with minimal environmental impact.

## Revenue Logic

To sustain the venture, the team is planning to explore a tiered subscription model. ## One-Sentence Summary

A lightweight, privacy-first tool that generates a verifiable fingerprint of a file and anchors it to a public, time-stamped ledger so anyone can prove originality and authenticity.

## Problem

The creators describe a digital landscape saturated with deepfakes, fake screenshots, copied artwork, and altered media, where "anyone can post anything," making it increasingly difficult to establish what's original or real. They frame the central question as how people can credibly prove ownership or first possession of digital work amid pervasive manipulation and duplication. This challenge extends beyond establishing simple precedence to undermining trust in digital evidence within legal, journalistic, and commercial contexts, where the inability to definitively authenticate a file's provenance or creation time can have significant consequences.

## Solution / Product Overview

The product creates a digital fingerprint of a user's file and records proof of that fingerprint on a public, time-stamped ledger. The approach is presented as secure and privacy-preserving: users do not upload the actual file; instead, a compact fingerprint is used as a durable evidence marker. The creators position this as a quiet safeguard for digital truth that everyday people, as well as professionals, can rely on.

## Key Features & Capabilities

-   File fingerprinting: produces a unique hash of a file for proof of originality.
-   Public, time-stamped anchoring: writes proof to a ledger that can be independently checked.
-   Privacy-first design: only hashed fingerprints are stored, not the underlying files.
-   Lightweight implementation: designed to be efficient and resource-conscious.
-   Security emphasis: presented as "lightweight, secure, and built for the future."

## One-Sentence Summary
A privacy-first platform that generates verifiable digital fingerprints of ethical sourcing events and anchors them to a public, tamper-proof ledger, giving brands and consumers indisputable proof of provenance while protecting sensitive operational data.

## Problem
The venture plans to address a digital landscape saturated with manipulation and duplication, where it is increasingly difficult to establish what is original or real. The central challenge is enabling people to credibly prove the authenticity and first possession of a digital record—such as a donation confirmation or an impact report—amid pervasive skepticism and the ease of generating false claims. A specific, related challenge the venture plans to explore is the inefficiency and lack of standardization in the verification process for corporate matching gift programs, where employees often face cumbersome manual steps to prove their donations, creating friction that reduces participation.

## Target Users & Use Cases
The venture plans to target both brands seeking to validate their supply chains and consumers looking for trustworthy ethical goods. Primary scenarios include discovering vetted, ethical suppliers (from large, global operations to local, artisanal producers), verifying material provenance with confidence, and providing transparent proof of sourcing standards to end customers. A planned, specific target user group for initial outreach is procurement teams within consumer goods companies participating in sustainability certification or reporting programs, who need a streamlined way to discover vetted suppliers and provide indisputable proof of their ethical sourcing actions to qualify for certifications or meet regulatory requirements.

## Solution / Product Overview
The venture plans to build a tool that creates a digital fingerprint of a user's charitable event data—such as a donation receipt or a nonprofit's impact milestone—and records proof of that fingerprint on a public, time-stamped ledger. The approach is proposed to be secure and privacy-preserving: users would not upload sensitive raw data; instead, a compact fingerprint would serve as durable evidence. This is positioned as a quiet safeguard for digital truth that donors, nonprofits, and verifying partners can rely on. To increase trust, the team plans to explore adding a decentralized, community-run witness network to provide a secondary, distributed layer of attestation to the ledger's timestamps. Furthermore, the venture plans to explore the development of a simple, open-source verification widget that any third-party website (like a nonprofit's blog or a corporate social responsibility page) could embed to allow visitors to independently verify the timestamp and authenticity of a claimed charitable fingerprint directly from that page.

## Delivery Model
The venture plans a lightweight, privacy-first implementation. It would not store sensitive donor financial details or the underlying event files. Instead, it would generate compact, anonymized fingerprints of that data for ledger anchoring. The goal is a secure and resource-conscious design that maintains long-term verifiability with minimal infrastructure. The team plans to build a clean public interface and supporting infrastructure to make the verification tools usable by its target audience.

## Revenue Logic
The venture plans to generate revenue through the sale of a subscription service to brands and suppliers for access to the verification ledger, advanced provenance reporting features, and supply chain management capabilities within the platform. An initial free trial period for the subscription service is proposed to onboard supplier and brand partners. The venture also plans to explore potential optional upgrades for enhanced connectivity or integration services for enterprise procurement or certification programs.

## Distinctiveness
The venture aims to differentiate itself by offering heirloom-quality goods that merge immediate, tangible material qualities with enduring digital proof of ethical sourcing. Its proposed system does not just provide a product; it aims to create a permanent, independently verifiable record that the materials were sourced according to specific ethical standards at a specific time, addressing the credibility problem of sustainability claims. This focus on verifiable provenance from a specific, ethical source is presented as a unique safeguard for both consumer trust and material truth. The venture plans to explore a further point of differentiation: structuring its public ledger not just as a tamper-proof record, but as a machine-readable, open registry of ethical sourcing standards. This would allow the fingerprints to be programmatically linked to the precise definitions of the ethical standards they claim to uphold, aiming to create a new layer of semantic clarity and auditability that is missing from current provenance claims.

## One-Sentence Summary
A privacy-first platform that generates verifiable digital fingerprints of ethical sourcing events and anchors them to a public, tamper-proof ledger, giving brands and consumers indisputable proof of provenance while protecting sensitive operational data.

## Problem
The venture plans to address a digital landscape saturated with manipulation and duplication, where it is increasingly difficult to establish what is original or real. The central challenge is enabling people to credibly prove the authenticity and first possession of a digital record—such as a donation confirmation or an impact report—amid pervasive skepticism and the ease of generating false claims. A specific, related challenge the venture plans to explore is the inefficiency and lack of standardization in the verification process for corporate matching gift programs, where employees often face cumbersome manual steps to prove their donations, creating friction that reduces participation.

## Target Users & Use Cases
The venture plans to target both brands seeking to validate their supply chains and consumers looking for trustworthy ethical goods. Primary scenarios include discovering vetted, ethical suppliers (from large, global operations to local, artisanal producers), verifying material provenance with confidence, and providing transparent proof of sourcing standards to end customers. A planned, specific target user group for initial outreach is procurement teams within consumer goods companies participating in sustainability certification or reporting programs, who need a streamlined way to discover vetted suppliers and provide indisputable proof of their ethical sourcing actions to qualify for certifications or meet regulatory requirements.

## Solution / Product Overview
The venture plans to build a tool that creates a digital fingerprint of a user's charitable event data—such as a donation receipt or a nonprofit's impact milestone—and records proof of that fingerprint on a public, time-stamped ledger. The approach is proposed to be secure and privacy-preserving: users would not upload sensitive raw data; instead, a compact fingerprint would serve as durable evidence. This is positioned as a quiet safeguard for digital truth that donors, nonprofits, and verifying partners can rely on. To increase trust, the team plans to explore adding a decentralized, community-run witness network to provide a secondary, distributed layer of attestation to the ledger's timestamps. Furthermore, the venture plans to explore the development of a simple, open-source verification widget that any third-party website (like a nonprofit's blog or a corporate social responsibility page) could embed to allow visitors to independently verify the timestamp and authenticity of a claimed charitable fingerprint directly from that page.

## Delivery Model
The venture plans a lightweight, privacy-first implementation. It would not store sensitive donor financial details or the underlying event files. Instead, it would generate compact, anonymized fingerprints of that data for ledger anchoring. The goal is a secure and resource-conscious design that maintains long-term verifiability with minimal infrastructure. The team plans to build a clean public interface and supporting infrastructure to make the verification tools usable by its target audience.

## Revenue Logic
The venture plans to generate revenue primarily through a transaction-based model, charging a fee for each verifiable digital fingerprint anchored to the public ledger. This would apply to brands, suppliers, and potentially other entities (such as nonprofits in the matching gift use case) seeking to create a permanent, auditable record of an ethical sourcing event or donation. The venture also plans to explore offering optional subscription tiers for high-volume users, which could include advanced provenance reporting features, supply chain management capabilities, or enhanced integration services for enterprise procurement or certification programs. An initial free trial period for generating a limited number of fingerprints is proposed to onboard partners.

## Distinctiveness
The venture aims to differentiate itself by offering heirloom-quality goods that merge immediate, tangible material qualities with enduring digital proof of ethical sourcing. Its proposed system does not just provide a product; it aims to create a permanent, independently verifiable record that the materials were sourced according to specific ethical standards at a specific time, addressing the credibility problem of sustainability claims. This focus on verifiable provenance from a specific, ethical source is presented as a unique safeguard for both consumer trust and material truth. The venture plans to explore a further point of differentiation: structuring its public ledger not just as a tamper-proof record, but as a machine-readable, open registry of ethical sourcing standards. This would allow the fingerprints to be programmatically linked to the precise definitions of the ethical standards they claim to uphold, aiming to create a new layer of semantic clarity and auditability that is missing from current provenance claims.

## One-Sentence Summary
A privacy-first platform that creates permanent, verifiable digital fingerprints of ethical sourcing events anchored to a public ledger, building an interactive ecosystem with educational tools for supply chain professionals and impact-focused organizations.

## Problem
The venture plans to address a digital landscape saturated with manipulation and duplication, where it is increasingly difficult to establish what is original or real. The central challenge is enabling people to credibly prove the authenticity and first possession of a digital record—such as a donation confirmation or an impact report—amid pervasive skepticism and the ease of generating false claims. A specific, related challenge the venture plans to explore is the inefficiency and lack of standardization in the verification process for corporate matching gift programs, where employees often face cumbersome manual steps to prove their donations, creating friction that reduces participation.

## Target Users & Use Cases
The venture plans to target both brands seeking to validate their supply chains and consumers looking for trustworthy ethical goods. A planned, specific target user group for initial outreach is procurement teams within consumer goods companies participating in sustainability certification or reporting programs, who need a streamlined way to discover vetted suppliers and provide indisputable proof of their ethical sourcing actions to qualify for certifications or meet regulatory requirements. The venture also plans to explore targeting non-governmental organizations (NGOs) and impact-focused philanthropic foundations as a distinct user group, who require independently verifiable, tamper-proof records of field-level sourcing events and material provenance to substantiate their impact reports to donors, auditors, and grant-making bodies.

## Solution / Product Overview
The venture plans to build a tool that creates a digital fingerprint of a user's charitable event data—such as a donation receipt or a nonprofit's impact milestone—and records proof of that fingerprint on a public, time-stamped ledger. The approach is proposed to be secure and privacy-preserving: users would not upload sensitive raw data; instead, a compact fingerprint would serve as durable evidence. This is positioned as a quiet safeguard for digital truth that donors, nonprofits, and verifying partners can rely on. To increase trust, the team plans to explore adding a decentralized, community-run witness network to provide a secondary, distributed layer of attestation to the ledger's timestamps. Furthermore, the venture plans to explore the development of a simple, open-source verification widget that any third-party website (like a nonprofit's blog or a corporate social responsibility page) could embed to allow visitors to independently verify the timestamp and authenticity of a claimed charitable fingerprint directly from that page.

## Delivery Model
The venture plans a lightweight, privacy-first implementation. It would not store sensitive donor financial details or the underlying event files. Instead, it would generate compact, anonymized fingerprints of that data for ledger anchoring. The goal is a secure and resource-conscious design that maintains long-term verifiability with minimal infrastructure. The team plans to build a clean public interface and supporting infrastructure to make the verification tools usable by its target audience.

## Revenue Logic
The venture plans to generate revenue through the sale of a subscription service to brands and suppliers for access to the verification ledger, advanced provenance reporting features, and supply chain management capabilities within the platform. An initial free trial period for the subscription service is proposed to onboard supplier and brand partners. The venture also plans to explore potential optional upgrades for enhanced connectivity or integration services for enterprise procurement or certification programs.

## Distinctiveness
The venture aims to differentiate itself by offering heirloom-quality goods that merge immediate, tangible material qualities with enduring digital proof of ethical sourcing. Its proposed system does not just provide a product; it aims to create a permanent, independently verifiable record that the materials were sourced according to specific ethical standards at a specific time, addressing the credibility problem of sustainability claims. This focus on verifiable provenance from a specific, ethical source is presented as a unique safeguard for both consumer trust and material truth. The venture plans to explore a further point of differentiation: structuring its public ledger not just as a tamper-proof record, but as a machine-readable, open registry of ethical sourcing standards. This would allow the fingerprints to be programmatically linked to the precise definitions of the ethical standards they claim to uphold, aiming to create a new layer of semantic clarity and auditability that is missing from current provenance claims. Furthermore, the venture plans to explore a distinctive approach by designing its ledger and verification tools to be purposefully protocol-agnostic, meaning it is engineered to interface seamlessly with multiple existing and emerging public ledger technologies and supply chain data formats, rather than locking the ecosystem into a single proprietary blockchain or system. This planned agnosticism is intended to future-proof the verification records and lower barriers to adoption for partners who may have existing technical infrastructures or preferences.
