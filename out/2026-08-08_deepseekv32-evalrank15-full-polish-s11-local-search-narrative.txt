* Step 0: Initial state

## One-Sentence Summary
A rugged, portable power system that generates verifiable digital fingerprints of its assembly, component sourcing, and operational milestones, anchoring them to a tamper-proof ledger to provide indisputable proof of ethical manufacturing and durable performance for off-grid users.

## Problem
The venture plans to address the market for portable power solutions that are often fragile, plastic, and not built for real-world stress, leaving people vulnerable during disasters or when living or traveling off the grid. The central challenge is providing reliable backup power for "freedom seekers," homesteaders, road-trippers, and anyone preparing for uncertainty, while also combating a digital landscape where claims about manufacturing quality, ethical sourcing, and durability are difficult to verify credibly.

## Target Users & Use Cases
The venture plans to target RV travelers, tiny-home residents, homesteaders, and preparedness-minded users, as well as anyone needing dependable emergency backup power. A planned, specific target user group for initial outreach is those who "live beyond the grid" and want durable, industrial-grade power on the go, who also value transparency about the provenance and assembly of the critical equipment they rely upon.

## Solution / Product Overview
The venture plans to build a tool that creates a digital fingerprint of a unit's assembly data—such as component sourcing records, quality test results, and assembly milestones—and records proof of that fingerprint on a public, time-stamped ledger. The approach is proposed to be secure and privacy-preserving: sensitive supplier or manufacturing data would not be uploaded; instead, a compact fingerprint would serve as durable evidence of ethical production and build quality. This is positioned as a quiet safeguard for product truth that end-users and verifying partners can rely on. To increase trust, the team plans to explore adding a decentralized, community-run witness network to provide a secondary, distributed layer of attestation to the ledger's timestamps. Furthermore, the venture plans to explore the development of a simple, open-source verification widget that any third-party website (like a dealer's page or a user's blog) could embed to allow visitors to independently verify the timestamp and authenticity of a claimed product fingerprint directly. Additionally, the venture plans to explore a feature where multiple related fingerprints (e.g., a component certification, an assembly log, and a performance test) could be linked into a single, verifiable provenance bundle for each physical unit.

## Delivery Model
The venture plans a lightweight, privacy-first implementation for the digital verification layer. It would not store sensitive supplier or manufacturing operational files. Instead, it would generate compact, anonymized fingerprints of that data for ledger anchoring. The goal is a secure and resource-conscious digital design that maintains long-term verifiability with minimal infrastructure, complementing the physical product. The team plans to build a clean public interface and supporting digital infrastructure to make the verification tools usable by its target audience.

## Revenue Logic
The venture plans to generate revenue through the sale of a subscription service to dealers, fleet operators, and enterprise customers for access to the verification ledger, advanced provenance reporting features, and asset management capabilities for their fleets of power units. An initial free trial period for the subscription service is proposed to onboard initial partners. The venture also plans to explore potential optional upgrades for enhanced connectivity or integration services for large-scale preparedness programs or commercial off-grid operations.

## Distinctiveness
The venture aims to differentiate itself by offering industrial-grade power goods that merge immediate, tangible ruggedness with enduring digital proof of ethical manufacturing and assembly. Its proposed system does not just provide a product; it aims to create a permanent, independently verifiable record that the unit was built according to specific quality and ethical standards at a specific time, addressing the credibility problem of durability and sourcing claims. This focus on verifiable provenance from a specific, ethical manufacturing process is presented as a unique safeguard for both user trust and product truth in a critical category.

## Problem
The venture plans to address the market for portable power solutions that are often fragile, plastic, and not built for real-world stress, leaving people vulnerable during disasters or when living or traveling off the grid. The central challenge is providing reliable backup power for "freedom seekers," homesteaders, road-trippers, and anyone preparing for uncertainty, while also combating a digital landscape where claims about manufacturing quality, ethical sourcing, and durability are difficult to verify credibly.

## Target Users & Use Cases
The venture plans to target RV travelers, tiny-home residents, homesteaders, and preparedness-minded users, as well as anyone needing dependable emergency backup power. A planned, specific target user group for initial outreach is those who "live beyond the grid" and want durable, industrial-grade power on the go, who also value transparency about the provenance and assembly of the critical equipment they rely upon.

## Solution / Product Overview
The venture plans to build a tool that creates a digital fingerprint of a unit's assembly data—such as component sourcing records, quality test results, and assembly milestones—and records proof of that fingerprint on a public, time-stamped ledger. To illustrate the provenance timeline, the venture plans to define a specific first operational milestone to be fingerprinted: the "successful completion of a 72-hour full-load burn-in test." The compact fingerprint for this test would include a minimal, concrete set of data points to clarify technical transparency: total energy output (kWh), test duration (hours), and average internal temperature during the test (°C). This initial fingerprint would serve as durable, time-stamped evidence of a critical quality assurance checkpoint. The venture also plans to propose a concrete, minimal first set of additional assembly milestones to be fingerprinted, building a more detailed provenance timeline. These planned milestones include 'final torque verification of all external fasteners' and 'battery cell capacity matching test.'

The approach is proposed to be secure and privacy-preserving: sensitive supplier or manufacturing data would not be uploaded; instead, a compact fingerprint would serve as durable evidence of ethical production and build quality. This is positioned as a quiet safeguard for product truth that end-users and verifying partners can rely on. To increase trust, the team plans to explore adding a decentralized, community-run witness network to provide a secondary, distributed layer of attestation to the ledger's timestamps. Furthermore, the venture plans to explore the development of a simple, open-source verification widget that any third-party website could embed to allow visitors to independently verify the timestamp and authenticity of a claimed product fingerprint directly. As an initial test of this widget's utility and to gather user feedback, the venture plans to propose a specific, hypothetical trial with a single, popular off-grid living blog.

To further demonstrate the system's credibility, the venture plans to propose a specific, hypothetical trial where a single unit's fingerprint is independently verified by a trusted third-party reviewer in the off-grid living space, with their verification statement published alongside the fingerprint on the public ledger.

Additionally, the venture plans to explore a feature where multiple related fingerprints—starting with milestones like the burn-in test, torque verification, and capacity matching—could be linked into a single, verifiable provenance bundle for each physical unit. To clarify the tangible benefit to the end-user, the venture also plans to explore a feature where the digital fingerprint could be used to verify a unit's eligibility for a proposed extended warranty or service plan, directly linking digital proof of assembly and quality to a user's financial benefit. As a specific, hypothetical trial for this feature, the venture plans to propose a scenario where a user, by verifying their unit's unique fingerprint through a public portal, could unlock a discount on a service plan offered by a single, hypothetical partner, such as a regional off-grid equipment maintenance provider.

## Delivery Model
The venture plans a lightweight, privacy-first implementation for the digital verification layer. It would not store sensitive supplier or manufacturing operational files. Instead, it would generate compact, anonymized fingerprints of that data for ledger anchoring. The goal is a secure and resource-conscious digital design that maintains long-term verifiability with minimal infrastructure, complementing the physical product. The team plans to build a clean public interface and supporting digital infrastructure to make the verification tools usable by its target audience.

## Revenue Logic
The venture plans to generate revenue through the sale of a subscription service to dealers, fleet operators, and enterprise customers for access to the verification ledger, advanced provenance reporting features, and asset management capabilities for their fleets of power units. An initial free trial period for the subscription service is proposed to onboard initial partners. The venture also plans to explore potential optional upgrades for enhanced connectivity or integration services for large-scale preparedness programs or commercial off-grid operations.

## Distinctiveness
The venture aims to differentiate itself by offering industrial-grade power goods that merge immediate, tangible ruggedness with enduring digital proof of ethical manufacturing and assembly. Its proposed system does not just provide a product; it aims to create a permanent, independently verifiable record that the unit was built according to specific quality and ethical standards at a specific time, addressing the credibility problem of durability and sourcing claims. By defining clear initial milestones like a burn-in test (with specific data points like total energy output, duration, and average temperature), torque verification, and battery cell capacity matching test for fingerprinting, it aims to build a concrete and more detailed provenance timeline. The proposed trial of third-party reviewer verification aims to add a further layer of independent attestation. This focus on verifiable provenance from a specific, ethical manufacturing process is presented as a unique safeguard for both user trust and product truth in a critical category.
